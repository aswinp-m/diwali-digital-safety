TRY IT BEFORE YOU CLICK

Where does that link go?

Practice with fictional websites. These examples stay on this page.

Peek at the destination

On a computer, hover over a link without clicking. Read the address in the browser’s lower corner. On a phone, press and hold a link to inspect its address, then dismiss the preview.

PRACTICE MESSAGE

Your parcel is waiting. Confirm delivery here.

Track my parcel

Hover, use Tab to focus, or tap the underlined words.

The destination will appear here.

The label says “Track my parcel.” The address reveals a different destination. Compare it with the carrier’s known website before opening it. A shortened link can hide the final address.

Read the actual domain

Suppose your carrier’s known address is parcel.example. Which address belongs to it?

Choose an address to see who controls it.

Check the full address bar. A familiar logo, HTTPS, or a padlock alone cannot prove that a site is legitimate. A scam site can have all three. Open a saved bookmark, use the official app, or type the known address yourself. Stop at browser security warnings.

PASSKEY DEMONSTRATION

One passkey. The original service.

Your browser checks the site’s domain before using a passkey. Your PIN, face, or fingerprint unlocks the passkey, then your device proves it has the matching key. Your fingerprint stays on your device.

This simulation uses a passkey created for parcel.example. Choose a site, then try signing in.

https://parcel.example/sign-in

Simulation ready. No real account or passkey is used.

A lookalike domain cannot use the original service’s passkey. Services can also support their own approved related domains and apps. If a page unexpectedly asks you to switch to a password, stop and open the service through a route you already trust.

REPORT & RECOVER

Already shared something?

If money moved, call your bank or payment service immediately using its official number and ask whether it can stop or reverse the payment. Save messages, receipts, dates, and transaction IDs.
